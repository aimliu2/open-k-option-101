Options / Hedging / Visual Summary

Downside protection
≠ delta neutrality.

A protective put or collar usually keeps a bullish position. The aim is to cushion or cap losses—not eliminate every small directional movement.

The core idea: Retain positive delta while shaping what happens if the underlying falls. Delta measures local sensitivity; the option payoff defines the loss boundary at expiration.
01 / Protective put

Keep the upside. Buy a floor.

Δtotal = +1 + Δput

Own 1 BTC and buy a put with delta −0.30.

+0.70

Example starting net delta

You are still directionally long. As BTC declines, the put tends to become more negative in delta, reducing net exposure toward zero. On a strong rally, net delta can approach +1.

02 / Collar

Trade upside for a cheaper floor.

Δtotal = +1 + Δput − Δlong call
Long 1 BTC+1.00
Long OTM put−0.25
Short OTM call−0.30
Starting net delta+0.45

Still bullish, but with reduced downside sensitivity and capped upside at expiration.

03 / How net delta behaves

Protection changes as the price moves.

Conceptual illustration with a $90 put and $110 short call around a $100 starting spot price. Delta values are illustrative, not quotes.

Long spotSpot + putSpot + put − call
Reading the curves: A protective put makes net delta shrink toward zero on major declines but allows it to rise toward +1 on rallies. A collar can make net delta shrink toward zero at both extremes: the put protects the downside and the short call limits the upside.
04 / What delta tells you

Instantaneous sensitivity

For small moves, a +0.70 delta means an approximate $0.70 change in position value per $1 change in the underlying, per equivalent unit, all else equal.

Actual option values also change with gamma, volatility, and time.

05 / What payoff tells you

Defined downside boundary

A correctly sized protective put can establish a contractual loss floor at expiration even if the position's current delta is +0.70.

The premium, strike selection, expiration, and contract terms determine the actual maximum loss.

Bottom line

Buy and Hedge = bullish exposure + a downside cushion, not permanent delta neutrality.
